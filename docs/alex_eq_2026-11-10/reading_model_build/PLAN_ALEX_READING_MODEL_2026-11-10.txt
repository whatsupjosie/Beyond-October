B. THE MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH (check this for loops and wrong turns)
==========================================================================
ONE TURN, IN ORDER
  1. The person's message arrives.
  2. Is the whole message an exit word (stop, pause, exit, go lucid, step out of role)? If yes: stop. Nothing else runs. This is plain code with no model, so it works even if every model is down.
  3. Your existing keyword layer and affect engine run exactly as they do today.
  4. A language model fills in the form about the message: what happened; whose it is (theirs, someone close, someone else, fiction, a figure of speech); what they say about their own state; the tone; what they asked for; whether they corrected Alex.
     If the form is missing or broken, the turn is marked "meaning: unavailable". Alex does not guess. She goes on with what she already knew.
  5. Anything that happened in the person's own life goes into their private ledger with a weight. Each kind fades at its own rate: going without food in hours, a bad night in a day, a fight in a week, a death over weeks. Entries that fade to nothing are deleted.
  6. The evidence is added up by family: events, what they say about themselves, style (from your engine), voice, and their reaction to Alex's offers. Each family has a ceiling. Style or voice alone can reach "noticed" and never "strong". The camera adds nothing to the estimate.
  7. "I'm fine": if nothing else disagrees, it is believed. If the rest disagrees, it pulls only as far as your dial. Either way the estimate can never reach certainty.
  8. The gap: if what they say about themselves does not match the rest, or they are joking about something painful, or they have gone flat after something heavy, the reading says so. Alex never says so to the person.
  9. A correction ("No, I wasn't ashamed") drops the reading at once, stores "ashamed" as a reading never to use for this person again, keeps their own word, refunds the probe budget, and marks Alex as a possible cause.
 10. The move is chosen, in this order of precedence:
       exit word > a correction > one unclear name or thing > what they asked for > good news > they asked to be heard or for space > something new they just told Alex > "I'm not okay" > otherwise the ladder.
 11. Background rule: if something heavy is known but nothing in this stretch of conversation shows it, Alex carries on normally and follows their lead. A death last week must not make her tiptoe for a month.
 12. The ladder, only when weight is showing now and they asked for nothing:
       two turns of quiet adjustment > one small offer > (two more quiet turns) > one open question, only if the reading is still strong and you allow observation questions > quiet adjustment.
 13. A short reading card goes to the model that writes the reply. An outside model gets how sure, the move, the need and the rules. It never gets memory text. A local model on your machine also gets what is going on.
 14. The drafted reply is checked: does it tell them what they feel, offer a hotline nobody asked for, promise an outcome, pile up questions, cheer over something heavy, ignore an exit, or bring back a rejected reading. One repair at most.
 15. The reading is logged as a bet. Next turn, what the person does settles it: held, corrected, or unknown.

WHERE A LOOP COULD FORM, AND WHAT STOPS IT
  - Probing again and again: a budget of three. Refunds can never raise it above three. Annoying the person costs extra and silences all probing for three turns.
  - Offering again and again: one offer per episode. A "no" is final for the rest of the episode (your dial).
  - Asking again and again: one open question per episode.
  - Repairing a reply again and again: one repair, then the plain version is sent.
  - Holding back forever: after two quiet turns the ladder has to move.
  - An episode that never ends: entries fade, and the background rule releases Alex's behaviour as soon as the weight stops showing, even while the ledger still remembers.
  - A model that keeps proposing a rejected reading: the rejected list is written into the form's instructions, filtered out of what comes back, and caught again by the reply check.

A WEAKNESS I KNOW ABOUT
  Step 12 counts turns, not minutes. Two quick one-word turns move the ladder as far as two slow ones. Your engine already measures pace; when this is wired, the ladder should also wait on the clock. I left it as turns because I could test turns honestly today.
